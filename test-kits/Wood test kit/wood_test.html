Wood test — how strong are your 3 × 3 sticks?

The app's strength predictions are only as good as its wood data. The supplied sticks are an unknown species, so these three quick tests replace the book values with measurements. Do them on sticks from the same batch you'll build with.

You need

  • About 6 sticks, a 0.01 g scale, and a digital scale that reads to at least 25 kg (bathroom scale, luggage scale, or a heavy-duty kitchen scale). A school materials-testing machine is even better.
  • Printed helpers: squaring block, two crush pads, two buckling end caps, 150 mm cut guide (files in this folder).
  • Calipers if you have them, a fine saw or sharp knife, 240-grit sandpaper on a flat surface, a flat offcut of board to press with, and a friend filming the scale display (to catch the peak reading).

Test 1 — Density (5 min)

  1. Measure the stick size with calipers in a few places (nominal 3.0 mm).
  2. Weigh 5 whole sticks together and measure their length. Enter count, length, and total mass in the app.

Test 2 — Crushing strength (15 min)

  1. Cut 5 pieces a little over 12 mm long from different sticks.
  2. Push each piece into the squaring block and sand both ends flush on the sandpaper. That makes them exactly 12 mm with square ends — square ends matter more than anything else here.
  3. Put a crush pad on the scale, stand the sample in its pocket, put the second pad on top (pocket down), then lay the board on top. Zero the scale first with the pads on.
  4. Press down slowly and steadily (about 10 s to failure) with both hands flat on the board. The reading climbs, then stops climbing or drops as the wood crushes. Record the highest reading.
  5. Repeat for all 5. Expect very roughly 11 kg each if the sticks are medium balsa.

Test 3 — Stiffness by buckling (10 min)

  1. Cut 3 straight pieces exactly 150 mm long using the cut guide. Pick straight ones.
  2. Put one end cap on the scale (socket up), stand the stick in it, put the other cap on top, and press down gently through the board, keeping it vertical.
  3. The stick bows sideways and the reading stops rising. Record that steady reading (the buckling load) — don't keep pushing until it snaps. Expect roughly 2.7 kg for medium balsa.

Enter the results

In the app: Wood and glue → Wood test…, type in the numbers, check the warnings, click Apply. The app then uses your measured density, crushing strength and stiffness for every design (it still applies the 85% build-quality factor on top, because a glued tower is never as good as a clean sample).
