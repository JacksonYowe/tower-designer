Glue test — how much glue does your hand leave?

The app estimates glue per joint from each glue's viscosity and the joint's size, then multiplies by a hand factor for how you actually apply it. This test measures that factor for each glue, so the tower's mass budget is based on your gluing, not a guess.

You need

  • Per glue: one 100 mm length of Boyle 5 × 5 mm square rod (the "host") and 5 strips 2.5 × 3 × 30 mm — laser cut from glue_test_strips_1.dxf (labelled PV1…, CA1…, EP1…, BC1…; one spare each).
  • The printed jig: glue_test_jig_base.stl and glue_test_jig_comb.stl (optional but keeps every joint square).
  • A scale reading to 0.01 g (0.001 g is better), the glues, a toothpick per glue, a pencil.

Method

  1. Label each host rod with its glue (pencil). Keep each glue's host and strips together.
  2. Weigh before: put one host and its 5 strips on the scale together. Write the mass in the table. Do this for every glue.
  3. Set up: lay the host in the jig channel and sit the comb on its pegs.
  4. Glue each strip: put glue on one END of the strip exactly the way you will on the real tower (same toothpick, same size dab, same wiping). Push it down through the comb onto the host and hold it a few seconds. Don't add extra just because it's a test — the point is to copy your real habit.
  5. Wipe squeeze-out the way you would on the tower (or don't, if you won't).
  6. Cure fully (times below). Lift the comb off carefully. PVA and balsa cement lose water/solvent for a day — weigh again the next day and use the lower reading.
  7. Weigh after: the host with its 5 glued strips. Write it down.
  8. Enter the before and after masses in the app: Wood and glue → Glue test…, then click Apply. The app sets each glue's factor and every design is re-weighed with your real glue amounts.

Why the strip end goes onto the side of the rod: that is the most common joint in the tower (a brace or ring ending on a leg), and the end grain soaks up glue the way it will on the real thing. Do all glues on the same day, with the same wood, so they compare fairly.
